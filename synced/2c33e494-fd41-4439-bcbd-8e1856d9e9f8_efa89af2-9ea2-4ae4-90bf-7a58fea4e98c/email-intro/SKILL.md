# Email Introduction

Create professional email introductions in Dave's conversational style with proper double opt-in etiquette.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Check Double Opt-In Status

Ask: "Have you already gotten permission from both parties for this introduction?"

- **If YES**: Skip to Step 3 (write final introduction)
- **If NO**: Proceed to Step 2 (write permission requests)

### Step 2: Double Opt-In Permission Requests

Create TWO separate permission emails (one to each person).

**Permission Email Structure:**
```
Subject: intro?

[Greeting]

[Explain who the other person is]
[Why the connection makes sense]

Interested in email intro?

Dave
```

**Example:**
```
Subject: intro?

Hey Connor,

I just met a product guy with some pretty good automotive experience. 
https://www.linkedin.com/in/dangronsbell/

He was at Assurant up until recently. Now looking for his next gig.

Interested in email intro?

Dave
```

Send both permission emails, then STOP. Wait for user to confirm both parties agreed before proceeding to Step 3.

### Step 3: Final Introduction Email

Gather context about both people:
- Search Gmail for prior conversations
- Search LinkedIn if needed for professional background
- Ask user for additional context if needed

**Introduction Email Format:**

**Subject Line:** `[Name A] <> [Name B]`

**Body Structure:**
```
[Greeting]

[Person A], I'd like to introduce you to [Person B] ([LinkedIn URL]). 
[1-2 sentences: how you know them, their current situation/role, 
relevant background]

[Person B], [Person A] ([LinkedIn URL]) [1-2 sentences: their role/company, 
what they do, relevant expertise]

[Optional: 1 sentence explaining why this connection makes sense or 
any relevant context]

Hope you guys can connect soon!

Dave
```

**Greeting defaults:**
- Two men: "Hello gents!"
- Women or mixed gender: "Hello friends!"
- Can be customized based on relationship (e.g., "Hello chaps!")

## Writing Style Guidelines

### Do
- Keep it conversational and friendly
- Use specific, colorful details about people
- Include LinkedIn URLs in parentheses after names
- Keep total word count between 100-150 words
- Be genuine and human
- Use natural language

### Don't
- Use em dashes (—) - AI tell
- Use buzzwords: "digital transformation", "synergy", "leverage", etc.
- Copy LinkedIn profile language verbatim (e.g., "generated $400M+ in P&L value")
- Use marketing speak or excessive praise
- Be overly formal
- Go heavy on career accomplishments

### Tone
Write like a friendly colleague making an introduction at a casual networking event. Complimentary without being over-the-top. Professional without being stiff.

## Example Introduction

```
Subject: Connor <> Dan

Hello gents!

Connor, I'd like to introduce you to Dan Gronsbell 
(https://www.linkedin.com/in/dangronsbell/). We connected last week 
through a mutual friend. Dan just wrapped up at Assurant where he was 
VP of Global Innovation leading product development. He's looking for 
his next product leadership role in automotive and is based in Atlanta.

Dan, Connor Finnegan (https://www.linkedin.com/in/connorfinnegan1/) 
is Chief Strategy Officer at Spiffy here in the Triangle. They do 
mobile automotive service for dealers and OEMs. Connor's got an MBA 
from Fuqua and has been building out Spiffy's dealer solutions and 
corporate strategy.

Connor mentioned he's open to connecting even though Spiffy isn't 
currently hiring in the product space. Always good to meet talented 
product folks.

Hope you guys can connect soon!

Dave
```

**Word count:** 122 words
